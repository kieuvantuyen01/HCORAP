\section*{\textbf{MaxSAT-Based Home Care Optimization}: Lexicographic Objectives, Totalizer Encoding, and Constraint Enhancements}

\subsection*{1. Introduction}

Optimizing resource allocation in home care services is a combinatorial problem of growing societal importance as populations age and demand for in-home assistance surges  \cite{Unceta2024Optimizing}. The original paper by Unceta et al. frames this as a MaxSAT-based resource allocation problem, maximizing caregiver–user pairing similarity and consistency while minimizing costs  \cite{Unceta2024Optimizing}. MaxSAT has become a competitive generic approach for solving combinatorial optimization problems, partly due to new solving techniques and the development of efficient encodings  \cite{Li2021MaxSAT,} \cite{Bacchus2021Maximum}. The proposed improvements—lexicographic optimization, Totalizer encoding, implied constraints, and symmetry breaking—each draw on distinct research streams that span Boolean optimization theory, cardinality constraint encodings, and constraint propagation techniques.

Lexicographic optimization provides a structured way to prioritize competing objectives by ranking criteria in strict order of importance  \cite{Semenova2021LEXICOGRAPHIC}. In the MaxSAT context, lexicographic optimization is reducible to single-objective optimization via the weighted sum method, where multiplying the primary objective by a sufficiently large coefficient and summing with secondary objectives yields the lexicographically optimal solution  \cite{Jabs2024From}. The Totalizer encoding, a cardinality constraint encoding, has demonstrated strong performance in MaxSAT solvers, particularly when combined with sorting networks  \cite{Jahren2018Resizing}. Implied constraints—logically entailed by the problem formulation but made explicit—can significantly improve solver performance by guiding branching heuristics  \cite{Bofill2022The}. Symmetry breaking predicates impose ordering constraints on variables to eliminate redundant solution permutations, though complete symmetry breaking is NP-hard  \cite{Anders2024The}.

\subsubsection*{3.2 Lexicographic Optimization in MaxSAT and Combinatorial Problems}

Lexicographic optimization ranks objectives by strict priority, optimizing the most important criterion first and using less important criteria only to break ties  \cite{Semenova2021LEXICOGRAPHIC} \cite{Rentmeesters1996A}. In Boolean domains, Marques-Silva et al. developed algorithms for multi-objective combinatorial optimization with lexicographic criteria, building on MaxSAT, pseudo-Boolean optimization, and ILP, demonstrating significant performance gains over state-of-the-art solvers on haplotyping and software package dependency instances  \cite{Marques-Silva2011Boolean}. Crucially, they found that lexicographic optimization conditions are observed in the majority of problem instances from MaxSAT evaluations, motivating dedicated algorithms that exploit these conditions  \cite{Marques-Silva2011Boolean}.

In bi-objective MaxSAT, Jabs et al. formalized lexicographic dominance: a solution τ dominates τ′ in the lexicographic sense if it has lower cost on the primary objective, or equal primary cost and lower cost on the secondary objective  \cite{Jabs2024From}. They proved that lexicographic optimization is reducible to single-objective MaxSAT via the weighted sum method—multiplying the primary objective by a coefficient larger than the maximum possible cost of the secondary objective and summing them yields exactly the lexicographically optimal solutions  \cite{Jabs2024From}. SAT-based approaches for computing lexicographically optimal solutions by reducing to single-objective MaxSAT were previously developed by Argelich et al. and Marques-Silva et al.  \cite{Jabs2024From}.

For resource allocation specifically, lexicographic minimax approaches have a long history. Luss and Smith developed extremely fast lexicographic minimax algorithms for allocating resources among competing activities to balance weighted deviations from demands  \cite{Luss1986Resource}. Luss extended this to a general framework for equitable resource allocation, where a lexicographic minimax solution determines the lexicographically smallest vector of sorted performance function values, ensuring no activity can be improved without degrading an already worse-off one  \cite{Luss1999On}. Klein, Luss, and Smith applied lexicographic minimax to multiperiod resource allocation, repeatedly solving minimax problems of decreasing dimension to lexicographically minimize sorted weighted deviations  \cite{Klein1992A}. Abernethy et al. proved that minimizing an exponential loss function converges to a lexicographic maximum as the scaling parameter grows, providing a continuous relaxation pathway for lexicographic optimization  \cite{Abernethy2024Lexicographic}.

Demirovic et al., in their MaxSAT formulation for staff scheduling, explicitly suggested that developing a lexicographic optimization approach—first optimizing for the highest-weight under-coverage constraint and then optimizing the remaining soft constraints—would be valuable  \cite{Demirovic2017Modeling}. This directly parallels the planned improvement to the home care paper. Foschini et al. proposed anytime algorithms for lexicographic enumeration of the Pareto front, noting that lexicographic optimization is widely used in industrial contexts where decision-makers need only rank objectives by importance  \cite{Foschini2026Anytime}.

The Totalizer encoding enables incremental updates to cardinality constraints, which is critical for core-guided MaxSAT algorithms that iteratively extract unsatisfiable cores and reformulate objectives  \cite{Jahren2018Resizing}. Morgado et al.'s core-guided MaxSAT algorithm with soft cardinality constraints focuses on reusing encoded cardinality constraints rather than regenerating them, improving robustness on industrial instances  \cite{Morgado2014Core-Guided}. Demirovic et al. found that the intensive use of cardinality constraints in staff scheduling MaxSAT models was the primary reason results could not compete with integer programming, and emphasized the high significance of choosing good encoding strategies  \cite{Demirovic2017Modeling}. Bofill et al. demonstrated that PB(AMO) encodings—pseudo-Boolean constraints combined with at-most-one constraints—are substantially smaller than standard PB encodings, with solving time improved by more than one order of magnitude in some cases  \cite{Bofill2021SAT}.

\subsubsection*{3.4 Implied Constraints and Symmetry Breaking}

Implied constraints are logically entailed by the existing problem formulation but made explicit to aid the solver's branching heuristic. Bofill et al.—sharing co-authors with the original home care paper—demonstrated that the impact of implied constraints on MaxSAT solver performance depends on problem density (ratio of meetings to accommodation capacity) and shape (ratio of time slots to locations)  \cite{Bofill2022The}. For low-density instances, certain implied constraints (imp1 and imp12) solved instances faster  \cite{Bofill2022The}. The mechanism is that the solver's branching heuristic focuses on variables of the implied constraints, reducing the search space and solving times  \cite{Bofill2022The}.

Symmetry breaking predicates (SBPs) impose an order on variables, selecting the lexicographic leader in each orbit of assignments  \cite{Anders2024The}. Complete lex-leader SBPs are NP-hard to compute, so incomplete SBPs are widely used in practice  \cite{Anders2024The}. Anders et al. proved that efficient computation of complete SBPs faces a natural barrier: efficient certification of graph non-isomorphism, explaining the difficulty of obtaining short SBPs for matrix models with row-column symmetries  \cite{Anders2024The}. Bogaerts et al. developed a certification method for optimization problems where symmetry and dominance breaking are expressible, demonstrating efficient verification of fully general symmetry breaking in SAT solving  \cite{Bogaerts2022Certified}. Dominance breaking generalizes symmetry breaking: if every solution not satisfying an added constraint is dominated by another solution, the constraint can safely strengthen the formula  \cite{Bogaerts2022Certified}. Even for pure decision problems, one can invent a lexicographic objective and add dominance constraints with respect to that order to achieve symmetry breaking  \cite{Bogaerts2022Certified}.

Demirovic et al. noted that investigating symmetry breaking in their MaxSAT staff scheduling model would be a natural extension, as would the lexicographic optimization approach  \cite{Demirovic2017Modeling}. Cappanera and Scutellà exploited the symmetric cost structure of real home care instances by introducing valid inequalities to enhance their ILP models  \cite{Cappanera2015Joint}, demonstrating that problem-specific symmetry exploitation is feasible in home care contexts.

\subsection*{4. Discussion}

The corpus reveals strong theoretical and empirical foundations for each planned improvement. Lexicographic optimization in MaxSAT is well-established: the weighted sum reduction is proven correct  \cite{Jabs2024From}, dedicated Boolean lexicographic algorithms outperform generic solvers  \cite{Marques-Silva2011Boolean}, and lexicographic conditions are observed in the majority of MaxSAT evaluation instances  \cite{Marques-Silva2011Boolean}. The direct suggestion by Demirovic et al. to apply lexicographic optimization to staff scheduling MaxSAT—optimizing the highest-weight constraint first—provides a concrete methodological precedent  \cite{Demirovic2017Modeling}.

The Totalizer encoding evidence is robust but nuanced. Jahren and Asín Achá found that best performance was achieved when the Totalizer encoding was combined with sorting networks, but sorting networks should be used very sparingly  \cite{Jahren2018Resizing}—a practical insight directly relevant to encoding choices. Bofill et al.'s reduced generalized totalizer showed order-of-magnitude improvements  \cite{Bofill2021SAT}, suggesting the RGT variant may be worth considering alongside the standard Totalizer. The finding that no single encoding is universally best, and that efficiency depends on constraint characteristics such as coefficient magnitude  \cite{Bofill2021SAT}, means the experimental design should test multiple encodings.

Implied constraints have the most direct evidence base because Bofill et al. share co-authors with the original paper and study a structurally similar scheduling problem  \cite{Bofill2022The}. Their finding that the benefit depends on problem density and shape  \cite{Bofill2022The}means the home care instances should be characterized along these dimensions. The mechanism—branching heuristics focusing on implied constraint variables  \cite{Bofill2022The}—suggests the improvement is solver-dependent and should be tested across multiple solvers.

Symmetry breaking evidence is more theoretical. Complete SBPs are NP-hard  \cite{Anders2024The}, so only incomplete predicates are practical. The dominance-breaking framework of Bogaerts et al. generalizes symmetry breaking and can be applied even by inventing a lexicographic objective  \cite{Bogaerts2022Certified}, which connects directly to the lexicographic optimization improvement. Cappanera and Scutellà's exploitation of symmetric cost structures in home care ILP models  \cite{Cappanera2015Joint}demonstrates that home care problems do exhibit exploitable symmetry, though the MaxSAT encoding may introduce different symmetry structures than the ILP formulation.

\subsection*{5. Conclusion}

The literature provides strong theoretical foundations and empirical precedent for all four planned improvements to the MaxSAT-based home care allocation paper. Lexicographic optimization in MaxSAT is mathematically well-grounded through the weighted sum reduction, the Totalizer encoding has demonstrated competitive performance especially when combined sparingly with sorting networks, implied constraints improve solver performance by focusing branching heuristics, and symmetry breaking—while computationally hard in its complete form—offers practical incomplete predicates that can exploit structural symmetries in the problem.
