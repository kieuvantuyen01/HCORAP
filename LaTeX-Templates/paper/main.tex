\begin{abstract}
Home-care providers must assign every requested service to a qualified
caregiver at a feasible time.  A good schedule should maintain continuity of
care, limit overtime workload, and favor compatible caregiver--service
assignments.  The existing MaxSAT model combines these criteria in one weighted
score, which can obscure their priority.  We replace it with a continuity-first
lexicographic objective: minimize the number of additional caregivers, then
overtime workload, and finally maximize caregiver--service compatibility.  We
also compare sorting-network and Totalizer encodings and strengthen the model
with implied and symmetry-breaking constraints.  Experiments on the original
and corrected benchmarks separate objective and encoding effects and compare
the resulting objective values with Gurobi and CPLEX.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Home-care planning must match requested services with qualified caregivers and
feasible time slots.  Every request must be covered, caregivers and care
recipients must be available, and overlapping assignments are forbidden.
Schedule quality further depends on continuity of care, caregiver--service
compatibility, and overtime workload.  We study the assignment-and-scheduling
scope of the Home Care Optimal Resource Allocation Problem (HCORAP); routing is
outside this scope.

Unceta et al.~\cite{UncetaEtAl2024} encode HCORAP as weighted partial MaxSAT.\@
A weighted score is appropriate only when its coefficients represent the
intended trade-offs among criteria.  When continuity should take precedence,
lexicographic optimization expresses that preference directly: optimize one
criterion and use the next only to break ties.

The encoding also contains several counting constraints, including caregiver
workload and the number of caregivers assigned to related services.  Sorting
networks and Totalizers are two standard ways to translate such counts into
conjunctive normal form (CNF).  They can differ in formula size and solver
behavior~\cite{BailleuxBoufkhad2003,AsinEtAl2011}.  Implied constraints and
symmetry breaking offer two further ways to strengthen the encoding.

This work contributes (i) continuity-first and overtime-first lexicographic
objective rules for HCORAP; (ii) a Totalizer encoding augmented with two
domain-specific implied constraints and symmetry breaking for interchangeable
slots and services; and (iii) a paired evaluation on the original and corrected
benchmarks, including objective-value comparisons with Gurobi and CPLEX.\@
The evaluation addresses three research questions.  \emph{RQ1} compares the
schedule quality and solved counts of the weighted and
lexicographic objectives.  \emph{RQ2} compares the formula size, memory use, and
solved counts of sorting networks and Totalizers.  \emph{RQ3} measures the
individual and joint effects of the encoding enhancements.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Home-care optimization spans assignment, scheduling, routing, and uncertainty.
Joint models can coordinate caregiver assignment with schedules and
routes~\cite{CappaneraScutella2015}, whereas stochastic assignment models address
uncertain service requirements~\cite{ErrarhoutEtAl2016}.  These richer scopes
motivate important extensions, but they are distinct from the deterministic,
time-slotted HCORAP studied here.  Across this literature, continuity of care,
caregiver--service compatibility, workload, and overtime are established terms
for the schedule-quality criteria considered in our model.  In Boolean
optimization, weighted partial MaxSAT has supported staff
scheduling~\cite{DemirovicEtAl2019}, and Unceta et al.~\cite{UncetaEtAl2024}
give the MaxSAT formulation and benchmark on which our study builds.

Lexicographic Boolean optimization and multi-objective MaxSAT provide methods
for prioritized criteria~\cite{MarquesSilvaEtAl2011,JabsEtAl2024}.  Totalizers
and cardinality networks offer alternative CNF representations of counting
constraints~\cite{BailleuxBoufkhad2003,BailleuxBoufkhad2004,AsinEtAl2011}, and
cardinality structure can affect MaxSAT search~\cite{MorgadoEtAl2014,JahrenAsin2018}.
Implied constraints and symmetry breaking can further improve Boolean models by
exposing consequences of the formulation and eliminating equivalent labeled
solutions~\cite{BofillEtAl2022,BogaertsEtAl2022}.  We combine these techniques
for HCORAP and evaluate their interactions under prioritized objectives.

\section{Problem Formulation and Objective Rules}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{HCORAP model and quality measures}

The model contains caregivers, care recipients, requested services, time slots,
and \emph{continuity groups}.  A continuity group contains related services for
which using fewer distinct caregivers is preferred.  The benchmark files call
caregivers \emph{agents}, care recipients \emph{users}, and continuity groups
\texttt{SEQ}; these labels are mapped here to standard home-care terminology.
Each service occupies one time slot and contributes one workload unit.

For intuition, consider two services in the same continuity group.  Using one
caregiver for both improves continuity.  Using two caregivers may instead give
higher compatibility scores.  The weighted objective trades these changes
against each other, whereas the continuity-first objective fixes the best
continuity value before considering overtime and compatibility.

Formally, let $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal S$, $\mathcal H$, $\mathcal U$, and
$\mathcal Q$ denote the sets of caregivers, requested services, time slots,
care recipients, and continuity groups, respectively.  The compatibility score
$r_{as}\in\{0,\ldots,4\}$ measures how well caregiver $a$ matches service $s$;
the original study and the released files call this value \emph{similarity}.
A zero score means that the assignment is forbidden or the caregiver is not
qualified.  Availability, permitted service slots, and qualification define
the feasible triples
$E\subseteq\mathcal A\times\mathcal S\times\mathcal H$.  Variable $x_{ash}$ is
true exactly when caregiver $a$ performs service $s$ in slot $h$.  The summary
variable $y_{as}$ is true exactly when caregiver $a$ performs service $s$ in
some feasible slot:
$y_{as}\leftrightarrow\bigvee_{h:(a,s,h)\in E}x_{ash}$.

The mandatory scheduling constraints assign every service exactly once.  A
caregiver can perform at most one service in a slot, and a care recipient can
receive at most one service in that slot.  If
$L_a=\sum_s y_{as}$ is caregiver $a$'s workload, then
$L_a\le HN_a+HE_a$, where $HN_a$ is the regular-workload limit and $HE_a$ is
the permitted overtime limit.  Workload is measured in service units.

Let $\mathcal S_u$ and $\mathcal S_q$ be the services of care recipient $u$ and
continuity group $q$, respectively, and let
$n_q=|\{a:\exists s\in\mathcal S_q,\ y_{as}=1\}|$.  The measures are
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{SIM}  &= \sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S} r_{as}y_{as}, \\
  \mathrm{CONT} &= \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\max(0,n_q-1), \\
  \mathrm{OT}   &= \sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\max(0,L_a-HN_a).
\end{align}
Here SIM is the total caregiver--service compatibility score and is maximized;
CONT is the \emph{additional-caregiver count}, namely the number of distinct
caregivers beyond the first in each continuity group; and OT is the
\emph{overtime workload}, namely workload above each regular-workload limit.
Both CONT and OT are minimized.  These names are used consistently below.  The
original HCORAP stability reward, denoted STAB, satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{STAB}
  = \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}(|\mathcal S_q|-n_q)
  = \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}(|\mathcal S_q|-1)-\mathrm{CONT}.
  \label{eq:stability-continuity}
\end{equation}
Because every service is assigned, Equation~\eqref{eq:stability-continuity}
shows that maximizing STAB is equivalent to minimizing CONT.

\subsection{Weighted and lexicographic objective rules}

Let $p$ be the magnitude of the overtime penalty supplied with an instance.
The \emph{weighted objective} maximizes
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SIM}-w_c\mathrm{CONT}-w_o\,p\,\mathrm{OT},
  \label{eq:weighted}
\end{equation}
where $w_c,w_o\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ multiply CONT and OT; the primary experiments
use $(w_c,w_o)=(1,1)$.  The proposed \emph{continuity-first objective} solves
\begin{equation}
  \textsc{Continuity-first:}\qquad
  \min\mathrm{CONT}\ \longrightarrow\ \min\mathrm{OT}\
  \longrightarrow\ \max\mathrm{SIM}.
  \label{eq:lex-cos}
\end{equation}
The \emph{overtime-first objective} swaps the first two criteria and is used
only to test sensitivity to the chosen priority order.

The continuity-first objective is implemented in three stages.  Stage~1 proves
the minimum CONT value.  Stage~2 fixes that value and minimizes OT; Stage~3
fixes both values and maximizes SIM.  All stages share one time limit, and a run
is counted as optimal only when all three stages finish.

\section{Encoding and Constraint Enhancements}
\label{sec:encoding}

\subsection{Counting constraints: sorting networks and Totalizers}

The model must count caregiver workload and the distinct caregivers used in
each continuity group.  A cardinality encoding translates a bound on the number
of true Boolean variables into CNF.  The baseline uses a sorting
network~\cite{AsinEtAl2011}; the alternative uses a Totalizer tree with clauses
for both directions of each threshold relation~\cite{BailleuxBoufkhad2003,BailleuxBoufkhad2004}.
Both expose threshold
variables indicating whether a count reaches a given value.  These variables
represent workload and continuity penalties and also fix the optima of earlier
lexicographic stages.  We therefore compare the encodings empirically in terms
of formula size, memory, and solving performance.

\subsection{Implied constraints}

An implied constraint states a consequence of the scheduling model explicitly,
which may help the solver reject impossible choices earlier.  We add two such
constraints.

First, $t_{sh}$ is true exactly when service $s$ uses slot $h$, and $v_{uh}$ is
true exactly when care recipient $u$ receives a service in $h$:
$t_{sh}\leftrightarrow\bigvee_a x_{ash}$ and
$v_{uh}\leftrightarrow\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal S_u}t_{sh}$.  The service lists
partition services by care recipient.  Assigning every service, while allowing
at most one service per recipient and slot, therefore implies
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{h\in\mathcal H}v_{uh}=|\mathcal S_u|,
  \qquad \forall u\in\mathcal U.
\end{equation}

Second, let $G_h$ be the bipartite graph linking caregivers to care recipients
whom they can serve in slot $h$, and let $\nu(G_h)$ be its maximum matching
size.  The number of services scheduled in the slot cannot exceed this bound:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}t_{sh}\le \nu(G_h).
\end{equation}
Both constraints follow from mandatory service assignment and the caregiver and
care recipient non-overlap rules.

\subsection{Interchangeable values and symmetry breaking}

Two time slots are \emph{interchangeable} only when every caregiver--service
pair is feasible in both or in neither.  Two services are interchangeable only
when they belong to the same care recipient and continuity group and have the
same caregiver compatibility scores and caregiver--slot feasibility.  Swapping
interchangeable values preserves every mandatory constraint and all three
objective values.

Without symmetry breaking, the solver may explore several schedules that differ
only in the order of interchangeable values.  We impose a lexicographic order
within each detected equivalence class.  This removes duplicate labeled
schedules while retaining one representative with the same objective
values~\cite{BogaertsEtAl2022}.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Configurations, benchmarks, and campaign}

The \emph{baseline encoding} uses a sorting network without implied or
symmetry-breaking constraints.  The \emph{enhanced encoding} combines a
Totalizer, the two implied constraints, and symmetry breaking for
interchangeable slots and services.

To distinguish the effect of each change, we run a full $2\times2\times2$
factorial experiment: all combinations of two counting encodings, with or
without the two implied constraints, and with or without symmetry breaking.
The experiment covers 48 original-benchmark instances from 16 size classes and
three seeds per class.  The baseline--enhanced comparison reuses its two
corresponding configurations.

The objective-rule comparison uses the enhanced encoding.  The weighted and
continuity-first objectives are run on 42 original-benchmark instances from 14
classes and three seeds per class.

The \emph{corrected benchmark} (\texttt{corrected-v2} in the reproducibility
package) supplies feasible reference schedules and greater overtime variation.
Its evaluation set contains 48 instances from 16 size groups and three seeds per
group.  The weighted, continuity-first, and overtime-first
(OT${\to}$CONT${\to}$SIM) objectives use the enhanced encoding; the last tests
sensitivity to the priority order.  A 20-instance subset compares objective
values from EvalMaxSAT, Gurobi, and CPLEX.

\subsection{Protocol, measures, and analysis}

Experiments run on a Google Cloud \texttt{c4-highcpu-8} virtual machine with 8
vCPUs, 16\,GB of memory, and Ubuntu 24.04.  EvalMaxSAT, Gurobi, and CPLEX each
use one thread.  Every run has a 300\,s end-to-end timeout, shared by all
lexicographic stages.  Solver versions and commands are provided in the
reproducibility package.

We record formula size, runtime, peak resident set size (RSS), termination
status, and verified SIM, CONT, and OT values.  A run is solved when
the solver proves optimality or infeasibility.  Penalized average runtime
(PAR-2) assigns $2T$ to an unsolved run under timeout $T$.  Objective differences
and runtime ratios use matched pairs with verified optimal solutions.  We report
all factorial configurations and paired-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals for
median runtime ratios.  Size-class summaries are used to inspect heterogeneity.
Raw outputs, verification records, and scripts that
regenerate the tables accompany the paper.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The original benchmark contains limited overtime variation, so it may
understate differences involving OT.\@ The corrected benchmark supplies feasible
reference schedules and stronger overtime pressure, but it remains synthetic
rather than operational data.  Routing and uncertain availability remain
outside the implemented decision model.

The continuity-first implementation restarts EvalMaxSAT at each stage, so its
runtime includes the cost of staged solving.  Runtime results are specific to
the tested EvalMaxSAT version and hardware; Gurobi and CPLEX provide an
independent objective-value comparison rather than a runtime baseline.

Timeouts are retained in solved counts and PAR-2, while paired runtime ratios
use instances solved by both configurations.  Because the baseline--enhanced
comparison changes three encoding features together, individual effects are
drawn from the full factorial experiment.
